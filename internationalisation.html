    S03 · Situations — Container (row) › lead text | Icon List (6, dash bullets) · Text · Button "What I offer" → #offer · Image (full width, "Picture option")

    Are you responsible for internationalisation at your university or alliance? Perhaps one of these topics is on your mind right now:

    • Your university is active internationally on many levels, but there is no shared strategy that makes these initiatives visible, aligns them and ensures they have an impact.
    • You are building or strengthening a European Universities alliance under the European Universities Initiative (Erasmus+). You want it to become part of how your university works, not just manage it as a project.
    • You head an international office or coordinate an alliance and want to win support from university leadership and faculties for a shared internationalisation strategy.
    • You lead an EU project or consortium and want the collaboration to have a lasting impact beyond the project’s end.
    • You are looking for exchange and partnership models that fit your university’s strategy, for example within Erasmus+ or national programmes, or through virtual and blended mobility such as Blended Intensive Programmes.
    • You want international partnerships to work across languages and academic cultures, and not just exist on paper.

    In situations like these, I support you in giving internationalisation a strategic direction and building partnerships that benefit everyone involved.

    From my practice

    Rethinking internationalisation for a digital university

    Bifröst University in Iceland had moved to fully online teaching, but its internationalisation strategy still dated from before the switch. I helped the university’s leadership and international office develop new exchange models for digital learning and reorient its international partnerships. The university has since put both into practice.

    Read the full case study

    Building a European Universities alliance from the first idea: OpenEU

    OpenEU brings together ten universities from across Europe, most of them open and distance universities, to create the first pan-European open university. As Director Europe and Open Knowledge at the Universitat Oberta de Catalunya (UOC), I led the creation of the alliance. I brought the partner universities together and developed a shared vision with them across different national higher education systems, languages and academic cultures. Together, we agreed on how the partners would work together and what the alliance would offer students across Europe. In 2024, the European Commission selected OpenEU under the European Universities Initiative.

    International university partnerships: expert reviewer for Norway’s NORPART programme

    NORPART funds long-term academic cooperation and mutual student mobility between Norwegian universities and partner institutions in the Global South. As a member of the external expert panel, I assessed funding applications from university partnerships. Our assessments formed the basis for the funding decisions.

    Read the full case study

    Strengthening international cooperation strategically

    The Fundació Autònoma Solidària is the Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona’s foundation for social engagement and development cooperation. As project lead, I evaluated one of the foundation’s long-standing university partnerships together with a colleague. We examined what it had achieved, how lasting its impact had been and what could be transferred to other partnerships. The foundation has since used our recommendations to reorient its approach to international cooperation.

    Read the full case study

    Developing international research funding further: impact evaluation for the DAAD

    Since 1994, PROBRAL has funded joint research projects between German and Brazilian research groups. For the programme’s 20th anniversary, I was part of the team that evaluated it on behalf of the DAAD, as an associate of joyn-coop, in close dialogue with programme participants. The DAAD and Brazil’s CAPES implemented our recommendations to further develop the programme.

    How we work together

    Internationalisation

    I support universities in positioning themselves internationally and building their networks. Together, we develop an internationalisation strategy that fits your university’s overall direction, and we rethink partnership and exchange models. Growing digitalisation and rising sustainability requirements are changing the landscape. I work systemically and involve the people, committees and decision-making processes key to implementation.

    European Universities alliances

    I support European Universities alliances from set-up through to maturity, with a systemic and intercultural approach. The aim is reliable partnerships in which people enjoy working together. This is about more than project work: I help the member institutions build the alliance into their own strategy, structures and decisions, so that its impact reaches beyond individual funding cycles. This includes workshops with alliance teams, both at alliance level and in the member institutions, and work with alliance leadership on governance and transformation.

    EU projects

    I support EU projects and consortia whose change processes are meant to last beyond the project’s end. I help you carry the change from your project into your institutions and build strong collaboration within the consortium, across countries and cultures. This can include, for example, kick-off workshops and closing conferences.

    Frequently asked questions

    What makes a European Universities alliance sustainable after EU funding ends?

    Many alliances stick closely to what is written in the EU application and lose sight of the bigger picture. Institutional embedding succeeds when alliance work is understood not as a project but as a transformation process, both at alliance level and in the member institutions. I support this process through workshops at both levels and work with alliance leadership on governance and transformation.

    How can an alliance move from project to institutional transformation?

    The European Commission expects alliances to build deep institutional transnational cooperation, and many alliance teams are asking what that means in day-to-day work. Often, joint activities run alongside the member universities without changing how they work. The move from project to transformation starts when the member institutions ask what the alliance means for their own strategy, structures and decisions. That requires clear governance, people at each university who carry the alliance into their own institution, and regular reflection on what is actually changing. I support alliance leadership and member institutions in working on these questions together.

    How does internationalisation work at a university with a digital or hybrid learning model?

    Digital and hybrid learning formats change which international students you reach and what exchange can look like. Existing internationalisation strategies and exchange models often date from the era of purely on-campus study and no longer fit today’s reality. I work with your university leadership and your international office to develop new exchange models that fit the way you actually teach.

    How do we start working together?

    Every collaboration looks different, from a single workshop to longer-term support. In a free introductory call, we work out together what kind of support suits your situation.
