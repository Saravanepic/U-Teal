    What your university gains

    Clear priorities.

    You know which goals come first and where to put your limited resources. Whatever doesn’t belong, you choose to leave out.

    A strategy people stand behind.

    The people, committees and units key to implementation help shape it from the start. The result is a direction with broad support across your university.

    From paper to practice.

    Strategic goals become concrete projects with clear responsibilities. You test new approaches on a small scale before embedding them more widely.

    Keeping an eye on impact.

    Through regular reviews, you check what your projects are achieving. What works, you build on. What doesn’t, you adjust.

    S07 · How we work together (CSS ID offer) — H2 | Accordion (5 items, H3, closed)

    How we work together

    Strategy workshops

    I design and facilitate workshops in which your leadership team or your staff set strategic direction. There is room for different perspectives, and you leave with clear decisions, named owners and next steps. Formats range from a leadership retreat to a workshop with representatives of academic staff, administration and students.

    Supporting strategy development

    I support the development of strategies for universities, departments and research centres, or for specific areas such as sustainability, artificial intelligence or digital education. The process moves from an analysis of where you stand, to a shared direction, to a formally adopted strategy. I plan participation so that it is meaningful and the process stays manageable.

    Supporting implementation

    I help you anchor your adopted strategy in practice. Together we translate goals into concrete projects, clarify responsibilities and test new approaches in pilots first. That way, you can see early on what works and where you need to adjust.

    Impact assessment and participatory evaluation

    Together with the people involved, we look at what your strategy or programme actually achieves: what has changed, what works and where you need to adjust. Evaluation becomes a shared learning process rather than a box-ticking exercise at the end of a project.

    Internationalisation strategy

    I support universities in developing their internationalisation strategies and help European Universities alliances strengthen their cooperation.

    Frequently asked questions

    How can universities develop teaching strategically in the age of AI?

    Artificial intelligence is changing how students learn, how teaching staff assess and which skills graduates will need. AI guidelines alone aren’t enough to respond to this. The real question is what role your university wants to play in the future of education, and what that means for study programmes, teaching formats and assessment. This question can only be answered together with teaching staff, students and the relevant units. New approaches are best tested iteratively, using prototypes.

    How does sustainability become part of university strategy?

    Many universities already have a wealth of sustainability initiatives, often scattered across teaching, research, operations and student life. To turn these into a strategy, you first need an overview of what is already happening, and then a shared direction with clear priorities. Sustainability makes a difference when it shows up in decisions, budgets and processes, rather than being an extra task alongside day-to-day work.

    How do you develop a participatory strategic plan without the process dragging on forever?

    Participation needs a clear structure. It starts with the question of who is involved, when and for what: where do we gather perspectives, where do we work out solutions together, and where does university leadership decide? Well-designed formats such as large-group workshops or short surveys collect many voices quickly. A binding timetable, aligned with your governing bodies’ meeting schedule, keeps the process moving. This way, you get a strategic plan with broad support, without the process stretching over years.

    Why do strategies so often get stuck during implementation?

    In many cases, a strategy is written by a few people and then handed down to the rest of the university. Those who weren’t involved struggle to see what it means for their own work. Add to that too many goals without clear priorities, and unclear responsibilities. Implementation is more likely to succeed when the people who are meant to carry it out are involved early, when goals are translated into concrete projects, and when there are regular checks on what is working.

    What is participatory evaluation?

    In a participatory evaluation, it isn’t only external experts who assess what a project has achieved. The people involved reflect together on what has changed, what works well and what doesn’t. This brings out insights that an outside view alone often misses, and it makes people more willing to actually act on the results. For universities and European Universities alliances, it is a way to continuously improve strategies and programmes.

    How do we start working together?

    Every collaboration looks different, from a single strategy workshop to longer-term support. In a free introductory call, we get to know each other and see whether working together feels right for you. Then we clarify what you need, identify the people who should be involved and agree on a shared goal. I then send you a proposal with a timeline and cost estimate.
