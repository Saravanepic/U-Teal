    How we work together

    Coaching session

    A single coaching session helps when a decision needs clarity now, or when you want to reflect on a difficult situation before you act. You book sessions of 45 or 60 minutes, as often and at whatever intervals you need, with no fixed rhythm or package. Each session is devoted to exactly the topic you bring. I work according to the principles of systemic coaching and make sure you leave with concrete insights and next steps.

    Fees: 45 minutes €[price], 60 minutes €[price], [VAT note]

    Let’s talk

    Coaching programme

    A coaching programme supports you through a change that takes time. I stay at your side over several months, even when change doesn’t follow a straight line. Together, we design a programme over three to six months: regular sessions of 45 or 60 minutes, every two to four weeks. In addition, there is an asynchronous channel for urgent matters between sessions, with a reply within 48 hours. This way, the change in your leadership becomes visible, at the pace your situation needs.

    Fees: three months from €[price], six months from €[price], [VAT note]. The exact price depends on the number and length of sessions.

    Let’s talk

    The first 100 days

    When you take on a new leadership role, the first few weeks often set the course for how confident and effective you feel in the role later on. In a coaching programme for this phase, we look at how you lead, how your most important relationships develop and how your leadership plays out within the institution. For women, there is often another layer: structures and cultures that developed over decades without women in the room. Together, we look at how you move within them and where you want to lead differently, on purpose. The programme lasts three months, with sessions of 45 or 60 minutes every two weeks. It works best if you start shortly before taking office.

    Fees: €[price], [VAT note]

    Let’s talk

    Peer community

    In the peer community, you look for answers together with women in similar situations. Each group has no more than eight women, matched by level and career path and as similar as possible in language and culture. You meet five to six times, once a month. Each session focuses on one or two participants and their concrete questions. I hold the space for in-depth exchange using proven methods of peer reflection, such as the Case Clinics from Theory U.

    Fees: €[price] per participant for five to six meetings, [VAT note]

    Frequently asked questions

    What coaching is available for women in university leadership?

    Mentoring programmes, leadership seminars and traditional executive coaching are rarely tailored to your role and to the higher education sector. That gap is exactly why I created this offer. For women in university leadership, I offer four formats:

    • individual coaching sessions for a specific question or occasion
    • a coaching programme over three to six months for longer-term support
    • the first 100 days, a coaching programme for starting a new role
    • the peer community for exchanging ideas with other women in leadership

    All formats can be booked privately or by your institution. Together, we work out which one suits your current situation.

    What does coaching cost, and can my university pay for it?

    A single session costs €[price] for 45 minutes and €[price] for 60 minutes. A three-month coaching programme starts at €[price]. Many universities pay for coaching for their leaders, for example through staff development or the equality office. It’s worth asking there. I’m happy to prepare a proposal for your university.

    What is the difference between traditional and systemic coaching?

    Traditional coaching usually focuses on you alone, often on your behaviour or how you come across. Systemic coaching always works with you in the context of the system you interact with: you affect the institution, and the institution affects you. Together, we look at how you perceive things, what you want and how you act, and we look for solutions to your challenges within the system.

    How can I deal with committee politics and resistance as a woman in university leadership?

    Committee politics is easier to understand through a systemic lens than through behavioural training alone. Resistance rarely comes from individual people. It comes from the system in which everyone interacts: positions, history, unwritten rules. For women, there is often another layer, because many of these rules developed at a time when women were not in the room. You become effective once you look closely at how the system actually works, before you act. At the UOC, I sat on the senate as a representative of administrative staff and, as Director Europe and Open Knowledge, on the Research and Knowledge Transfer Committee. At the ACUP, I chaired committees with vice-rectors from the Catalan universities. I know these rooms from the inside, and I work with you concretely on your situation.

    What can I do about self-doubt in a new leadership role?

    Self-doubt in a new role is often not a personal weakness but an understandable reaction to a visible position with high expectations. First, it helps to tell whether the doubt comes from a real gap in knowledge or from an inherited picture of what leadership should look like. Instead of arguing the doubt away, it helps to pause and ask: what am I here for? That shifts the focus from “Am I good enough?” to what you want to achieve in the role. Small steps to try things out, supported by coaching or peer exchange, turn the new role into a learning process rather than a test.

    How does Theory U help me make clearer decisions as a leader?

    Theory U is a model for fundamental change in individuals and organisations, developed by Otto Scharmer at MIT. The core idea: the quality of a decision depends on the inner state from which you make it. If you pause and truly perceive what is happening, you can act from what the situation really needs rather than from old patterns. In coaching, this means we pause before you decide, look at your situation from different angles and clarify what really matters to you. That way, you see more clearly what is needed, even in complex situations.

    I barely have time as it is. How am I supposed to fit coaching into my schedule?

    Especially when your diary is full, a regular space to pause can make a big difference. The sessions are deliberately compact and flexible in frequency, and together we find a rhythm that fits your life. Clients tell me these hours feel like time well spent, because afterwards they see more clearly and decide more easily.

    How does online coaching work, and is it as effective as meeting in person?

    What counts in our work is the quality of the connection between us, not where we are. I work online with the same methods and the same attention as in person. We meet on Jitsi, and I send you the link in good time. You need a stable internet connection, a working camera and a place where you won’t be disturbed. Because movement, writing and drawing also have a place in our work, some space around you and a pen and paper help. If you’d rather not spend another hour in front of a screen, we can talk by phone, for example while you take a walk. Voice messages and shorter sessions are possible too.
