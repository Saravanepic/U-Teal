    S03 · Intro — Container (row) › Text Editor (lead style) · Text Editor · Heading (teal closing line)

    New technologies are changing teaching, research and administration at ever shorter intervals. At the same time, budgets are getting tighter. Universities have to respond to mounting pressure to change: what is planned today may already be out of date by the time it is put into practice.

    And yet universities depend on committees, participation and the different cultures of academic and administrative staff. How can they become more agile without giving up participation and careful decision-making?

    I work with universities across Europe and with European Universities alliances to plan and carry out transformation projects. I support leaders and build the kind of collaboration that brings change into day-to-day work. I have worked in and with universities since 2007, and I combine that experience with systemic approaches to strategy and organisational development.

    I work on structures and processes and, at the same time, with the people who shape them. Because change works when both change together.

    About

    Higher education experience and methodological expertise

    U-teal is a consultancy specialising in universities, with a focus on internationalisation, strategy, organisational development and leadership. I’m Nadja Gmelch, the founder of U-teal, and I support university leaders, teams and European Universities alliances through internationalisation, strategic and organisational change.

    I spent 15 years working at universities and in university networks, most recently as Director Europe and Open Knowledge at the Universitat Oberta de Catalunya (UOC) in Barcelona. Among other things, I led the creation of a European Universities alliance and developed university strategies. Since 2014 I have advised universities and evaluated international programmes for funders such as the DAAD. I have sat on a university senate and on committees myself, so I know where friction arises between academics, administration and leadership. I also know what it takes for people to work together constructively, and with joy, across disciplines, countries and languages.

    I combine this experience with systemic consulting and methods for designing strategy and change processes. That lets me keep both in view: the substantive questions your university is facing, and the people, structures and decision-making paths that determine whether things actually get implemented.

    I work in English, German, Spanish, Italian and Catalan. For larger projects, I work with colleagues whose experience complements mine. I come to you as often as your project needs: for retreats, workshops and important meetings. What works well online, we do online.

    Methodology

    How I work

    Before a university develops a new strategy or sets change in motion, it needs to understand where it stands. What is the real challenge? Whose perspectives need to be included? What exactly should change, and who needs to help shape the way there? And what attitudes and forms of collaboration will it take to make that change happen?

    I work systemically: I see a university as a web of people, committees, structures and relationships that all influence one another. And I pay as much attention to how people listen, talk and decide as to what they decide. Whether change succeeds also depends on how attentively we look and how far we include different perspectives. For this, I draw on Theory U. From these approaches, complemented by agile methods and design thinking, I have developed three steps for working with universities:

    1. Notice.

      We look closely at the current situation and actively listen to the different people involved. This brings assumptions, interests and possible obstacles into view.

    2. Align.

      We clarify what the university wants to achieve, which decisions this requires and what to tackle first.

    3. Act with impact.

      The shared direction turns into concrete steps. As we go, we continuously check what is working, where to adjust and how the changes can last across the university.
